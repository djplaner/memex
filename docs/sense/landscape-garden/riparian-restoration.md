Notes and resources dealing with land adjoining a body of water. Mostly related to the riparian parts of the [[gatton-creek-frontage]], [[roundabout]], and [[rocky-creek-frontage]] zones of [[wood-duck-meadows]]

Higher moisture levels and more fertile soil means riparian regions often differ in the diversity and density of fauna.

LfW note on RE 12.3.7 describes the geography of stream channels in SEQ as

- _bed_ - where the normal water flow occurs
- _fringing levee_ - flattish, narrow areas slightly above the bed that are inundated by floods
- _banks_ - higher areas enclosing the channel.
- _alluvial plains_ - flank the banks

## Related plants

Riparian forest section of SE Qld land types book suggest

- Mat rush (Lomandra longifolia, L. hystrix), 
- Blueberry lily (Dianella caerulea).

LfW note on 12.3.7 lists 

- dominant trees: Queensland Blue Gum, River Oak (Casuarina cunninghamiana subsp cunninghamiana), Weeping Bottlebrush (Melaleuca viminalis)
- low lying levees fringing the stream bed: Black tea-tree (Melaleuca bracteata), Broad-leaved apple (Angophora subvelutina), Creek sandpaper fig ([[ficus-coronata]]), Black Bean (Casstanospermum australe), Brown Laural (Cryptocarya triplinervis), Brush Cherry (Syzgium australe), Weeping Satinash (Syzgium floribundum)
- raised stream banks: Blue gum and Moreton Bay Ash (Corymbia tessellaris) broad-leaved and rough-barked apple and small clumps of rainforest species - Red Kamala, Black Bean, Silky Oak.
- River Oak often colonises gravel beds deposited by floods
- Ground layer is usually fairly open especially with dense shade: Matrush (Lomandra hystrix) and short creeping and spreading grasses and herbs

## Invasive species

Riparian environments are susceptible to invasion. Our stretch include the following

- Chinese Elm (Celtis sinensis)
- Lantana
- Castor Oil tree
- Leucaena
- Large-leaved privet
- Cats Claw Creeper
- Madeira Vine
- Green Panic
- Glycine
