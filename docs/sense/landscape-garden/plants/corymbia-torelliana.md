[Cadaghi (Coymbia Torelliana)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corymbia_torelliana) is a species of tree native to North Queensland which is considered an [environmental pest](https://weeds.brisbane.qld.gov.au/weeds/cadaghi) in South East Queensland. A lovely large tree with a dense canopy providing good shade. 

## Problems with Cadaghi trees?

In Southern Queensland, the Cadaghi is considered problematic. The Brisbane City Council weed identification site [describes the Cadaghi] as "an environmental week in SE Queensland and as a potential environmental weed in NSW". This is due to the combination of its dense canopy that in more open woodlands (e.g. more common in SE Qld) can limit native understorey plants and the Cadaghi producing resin-coated seeds which are spread by native bees in the same open woodlands.

There is further [concern for native bees](https://tweedvalleytreeservices.com.au/invasive-tree-species/the-cadaghi-tree/) due to the resin covered seeds. On return to the hives it is feared that the seeds can block entrances raising heat within the hive. Also that fumes from the resin during higher temperatures can then kill the bees. Here's [another report of the problem](https://zabel.com.au/australian-stingless-native-bees/the-dreaded-cadagi-tree/).

However, there is at least [one contrary opinion](https://www.qnc.org.au/2010/07/17/the-curious-relationship-between-the-cadaghi-tree-and-native-stingless-bees/), which at leasts suggests that the resin/heat problem is incorrect. Citing experiments suggesting bees die from the heat prior to the temperature required for the resin to melt.

## Wood duck meadows
