The main task today was clearing up part of the boundary that runs under a [[geijera-salicifolia|scrub wilga]] that is in turn growing under some enormous as yet unidentified gum tree. The section to be cleared had two clear parts to be cleared.

The first more westerly part was a good collection of quite old (and thick) [[asparagus-africanus]] with the odd bit of [[solanum-seaforthianum]]. The climbing asparagus was just starting to flower hence a good time for removal. 

This was a section where I'd previously removed asparagus, including some growing in the trees just visible on the other side of the fence.

<figure markdown>
![Fence covered with climbing asparagus](https://djon.es/assets/memex/sense/landscape-garden/work-history/2026/bunker-asparagus-before.jpeg)
<caption>Fence covered with climbing asparagus</caption>
</figure>

Thankfully the recent rain made removal a fair bit easier, though it was still a pain due to the chicken wire fence that was embedded into the soil (whether original intent or through accumulation over the years) making removing the asparagus crowns a challenge. For some the crowns remained with the stems being nipped and sprayed with roundup.


<figure markdown>
![After removal of the asparagus](https://djon.es/assets/memex/sense/landscape-garden/work-history/2026/bunker-asparagus-after.jpeg)
<caption>After removal of the asparagus</caption>
</figure>


The second more easterly section was almost entirely a very established collection of [[solanum-seaforthianum]].

<figure markdown>
![Fence covered with Brazilian Nightshade](https://djon.es/assets/memex/sense/landscape-garden/work-history/2026/bunker-nightshade-before.jpeg)
<caption>Fence covered with Brazilian Nightshade</caption>
</figure>

Again, some was fairly easily removed with others requiring roundup. With the nightshade removed the bunker is now visible in the background.

![Fence after removal of the Brazilian Nightshade](https://djon.es/assets/memex/sense/landscape-garden/work-history/2026/bunker-nightshade-after.jpeg)

#### Bare ficus

Whilst in the area, the bunker's parasitic ficus is still hanging on. It's gone through a phase of good growth, including hosting 3 or 4 paper wasp nests. However, currently it appears to be in a bit of a shutdown. All leaves shown in the above image are gone, but there is evidence of life with new growth appearing at the branch tips.

On the aesthetic front, I found this image quite striking once seen on a bigger screen. The weathered whites and greys of the concrete causing the ficus to almost disappear.
