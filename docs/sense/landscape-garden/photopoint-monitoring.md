title: Photopoint monitoring
---

The process of taking repeated photos from the same viewpoint to track [[regeneration]] progress (and also the impact of climate and natural events). Information the design of [[wood-duck-photopoint-monitoring]]

## Method

Defining goals of monitoring suggested as important first step.

DWER requirements

1. the location is secure - low risk from natural or human‑made disturbances 
2. the location can be easily located, now and into the future 
3. the photopoint is set up in a way that enables desired photos to be taken consistently over time

[ARRC how to](https://riversofcarbon.org.au/photo-monitoring-your-restoration-project-a-guide-for-landholders)

1.  Choose a photo point that provides a good, representative view of the project area
2.  Use an existing corner post, strainer post or install a freestanding post to mark the spot
3.  Keep in mind that plants will grow over time, stand far enough back from works
4.  Label every photo with the photo monitoring point #, date, any important information

Couple of suggestions to identify/record photopoints on a property mudmap.

## When?

[ARRC suggestions](https://riversofcarbon.org.au/photo-monitoring-your-restoration-project-a-guide-for-landholders)

1.  Before any work has started
2.  After land is prepared
3.  After initial planting/sowing/building
4.  At regular time intervals (1 year is best to catch the same season)
5.  After any natural disaster events (i.e. fire, flood)

## Photopoints

Common suggestion for each photopoint to be marked by fixed physical artefact, in particular posts. DWER suggests 2-3 posts

1. Photopoint post - sets the position and height from which to take the photo
2. Sighter post - Around 10m away from the photopoint post to set the direction and angle of the shot.
3. Spotter post - included in more difficult settings to help indicate whether the photopoint post is located.

Other advice suggests using a natural feature of the site to take on the role of sighter post.
